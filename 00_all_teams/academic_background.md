## Part A – The course references A1–A5 (verified)

### A1 · Kao & Huang – TAM in full- vs limited-service restaurants [PR]
- **Citation:** Kao, W.-K., & Huang, Y.-S. (2023). Service robots in full- and limited-service restaurants: Extending technology acceptance model. *Journal of Hospitality and Tourism Management*, 54, 10–21.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhtm.2022.11.006 (online December 2022)
- **OA:** closed; no legal OA copy found.
- **Summary** (from the abstract via Semantic Scholar):
  - An experiment on customer–robot encounters in two service contexts: full service and limited service.
  - Multi-group SEM: customers who perceive a high-quality interaction with the robot find it more useful, form more positive attitudes and feel rapport.
  - These effects are stronger in full-service than in limited-service restaurants. The service context changes how TAM works.

### A2 · Han et al. – drivers of robot acceptance in hotels [PR]
- **Citation:** Han, H., Kim, S. I., Lee, J.-S., & Jung, I. (2025). Understanding the drivers of consumers' acceptance and use of service robots in the hotel industry. *International Journal of Contemporary Hospitality Management*, 37(2), 541–559.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1108/IJCHM-02-2024-0163
- **Correction:** the course file says 2024. The article appeared online on 14.10.2024 and in print in the 2025 volume.
- **OA:** closed (Emerald pay-per-view).
- **Summary** (from the abstract):
  - Combines symmetric modelling (net effects) with asymmetric, configurational modelling of hotel guests' acceptance.
  - Significant drivers: innovativeness, willingness to be a "lighthouse customer", personal norms, and concern about robot performance.
  - The theoretical base combines TAM, the theory of planned behaviour, the norm activation model and sRAM. The paper also identifies consumer profiles such as tech-forward guests and uncertain or concerned guests.

### A3 · Begum et al. – meta-analysis of 56 hotel studies [PR]
- **Citation:** Begum, N., Faisal, M. N., Sobh, R., Rana, N. P., & Nunkoo, R. (2025). Consumers acceptance of service robots in hotels: A meta-analytic review. *International Journal of Hospitality Management*, 126, 104052.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhm.2024.104052
- **OA:** yes. The accepted author manuscript is available under CC BY 4.0 at Queen's University Belfast: https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/publications/consumers-acceptance-of-service-robots-in-hotels-a-meta-analytic-/
- **Summary** (from the abstract):
  - A weighted meta-analysis of 56 studies, written to reconcile inconsistent findings on why hotel guests accept robots.
  - Finds nine significant antecedents, grouped into functional, customer-centric and robot-centric aspects.
  - Functional and customer-centric factors are well researched; robot-centric factors need more research.

### A4 · Moriuchi & Murdy – factors affecting human–robot interactions [PR]
- **Citation:** Moriuchi, E., & Murdy, S. (2024). The role of robots in the service industry: Factors affecting human-robot interactions. *International Journal of Hospitality Management*, 118, 103682.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhm.2023.103682
- **Title correction:** the course file gives a shortened title.
- **OA:** closed; no legal OA copy found.
- **Summary:** **not verified.** The abstract could not be retrieved: ScienceDirect blocks automated access, and Crossref, OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar carry no abstract. Read the abstract at the DOI link before citing this paper. Do not rely on second-hand summaries.

### A5 · Çalışkan & Sevim – observational study with a leased robot [PR]
- **Citation:** Çalışkan, G., & Sevim, B. (2025). Use of service robots in hospitality: An observational study in terms of technology acceptance model. *Tourism and Hospitality Research*, 25(2), 167–179.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/14673584231198438 (online November 2023)
- **OA:** closed.
- **Summary** (from the abstract):
  - A leased service robot was shown in the lobbies, restaurants, rest areas and cafés of accommodation establishments. The data come from participant observation and unstructured interviews, coded in MAXQDA along TAM categories.
  - Behavioural responses: taking videos or photos, speaking to the robot, touching it. Cognitive and emotional responses: excitement, liking, having fun. On average 7 out of 10 users showed these reactions.
  - Method template for teams planning their own field observation: define observable behaviours first, then code them.

## Part B – Further key works

### B1 · Davis (1989) – Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) [PR]
- **Citation:** Davis, F. D. (1989). Perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, and user acceptance of information technology. *MIS Quarterly*, 13(3), 319–340.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2307/249008
- **OA:** closed.
- **What it contributes:** two six-item scales, perceived usefulness (reliability .98) and perceived ease of use (.94). Usefulness correlated more strongly with usage than ease of use, and ease of use may act through usefulness. TAM is the base model behind A1, A2 and A5.

### B2 · Wirtz et al. (2018) – service robot acceptance model (sRAM) [PR]
- **Citation:** Wirtz, J., Patterson, P. G., Kunz, W. H., Gruber, T., Lu, V. N., Paluch, S., & Martins, A. (2018). Brave new world: service robots in the frontline. *Journal of Service Management*, 29(5), 907–931.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1108/JOSM-04-2018-0119
- **OA:** open access at the publisher (hybrid OA according to OpenAlex).
- **What it contributes:** defines service robots and contrasts them with frontline employees. Introduces sRAM, which extends TAM with social-emotional and relational elements. Also discusses the ethics of robot-delivered service.

### B3 · Mavrogiannis et al. (2023) – social robot navigation survey [PR]
- **Citation:** Mavrogiannis, C., Baldini, F., Wang, A., Zhao, D., Trautman, P., Steinfeld, A., & Oh, J. (2023). Core challenges of social robot navigation: A survey. *ACM Transactions on Human-Robot Interaction*, 12(3), Article 36.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3583741
- **Preprint:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05668
- **OA:** free PDF at the ACM Digital Library and on arXiv.
- **What it contributes:** groups the open problems of navigating crowded public spaces into planning, behaviour design and evaluation methodology, and takes a critical view of current evaluation practice. Background for week 5.

### B4 · Macenski et al. (2020) – Navigation2 (Nav2) [PR]
- **Citation:** Macenski, S., Martín, F., White, R., & Ginés Clavero, J. (2020). The Marathon 2: A Navigation System. *2020 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS)*, 2718–2725.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/IROS45743.2020.9341207
- **Preprint:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00368
- **What it contributes:** the architecture paper for Nav2 on ROS 2, with behaviour-tree task orchestration. Nav2 ran safely among students on a university campus over a "marathon" distance. Cite this when you describe your stack.

### B5 · Kästner et al. – Arena-Bench and Arena-Rosnav
- **Arena-Bench [PR]:** Kästner, L., Bhuiyan, T., Le, T. A., et al. (2022). Arena-Bench: A benchmarking suite for obstacle avoidance approaches in highly dynamic environments. *IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters*, 7(4), 9477–9484.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2022.3190086
  - Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05728
- **Arena-Rosnav 2.0 [PP]:** Kästner, L., et al. (2023). Arena-Rosnav 2.0: A development and benchmarking platform for robot navigation in highly dynamic environments. arXiv:2302.10023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10023 (conference venue not verified)
- **Arena 4.0 [PP]:** Kästner, L., et al. (2024). Arena 4.0: A comprehensive ROS2 development and benchmarking platform for human-centric navigation using generative-model-based environment generation. arXiv:2409.12471. https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12471 (venue not verified)
- **What they contribute:** tools to generate dynamic worlds and scenarios, plus a metric suite for comparing planners. This is the source of the lecturer demo and the "hawker centre" world.

### B6 · Francis et al. (2025) – how to measure social navigation [PR]
- **Citation:** Francis, A., Pérez-D'Arpino, C., Li, C., et al. (2025). Principles and guidelines for evaluating social robot navigation algorithms. *ACM Transactions on Human-Robot Interaction*, 14(2).
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3700599 (online December 2024)
- **Preprint:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16740
- **OA:** yes (hybrid OA at ACM, and arXiv).
- **What it contributes:** eight principles of social navigation (safety, comfort, legibility, politeness, social competency, agent understanding, proactivity, responsiveness to context). Also gives guidelines for metrics, scenarios, benchmarks and simulators. Use it for the week-7 benchmark protocol.

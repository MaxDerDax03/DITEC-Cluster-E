# Anchor readings – read before 21.09.2026

Reading pack for all teams · status 13.09.2026 · about 1 hour

**Why these three.** Each piece shows a different way a customer-facing robot deployment can end.
Skylark is a **scale success**: 3,000 robots across about 2,100 restaurants, the largest rollout of its kind.
Chili's is a **pause despite happy guests**: staff liked the robot, yet it lost the internal competition for investment.
Henn-na is a **replacement failure**: robots meant to replace staff created extra work for them.
Keep these three patterns in mind when you analyse your own cluster.

## 1 · Chili's pauses its "Rita" server robots

- **Source:** [MR] Joe Guszkowski, "Chili's is pausing its test of server robots", *Restaurant Business*, 24.08.2022
- **Link:** https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/chilis-pausing-its-test-server-robots
- **Status:** OK. The full text loads; the site shows membership prompts.
- **Type:** trade press news article, based on Brinker International's fiscal Q4 2022 earnings call and a company statement.

**Key facts as stated in the article**
- Kevin Hochman, the new CEO, rated all technology projects on two things: their ability to drive sales and profits, and the time and effort they need. He told analysts: "The robotics project we're pausing right now".
- According to Brinker, some of the 61 Chili's restaurants that have a Rita will keep using it, but no new locations will get one.
- The Bear Robotics robots had been tested for "the better part of two years". After a successful first test in 10 restaurants, Chili's announced in April 2022 that 51 more locations would get Rita.
- Staff were increasingly using Rita for a range of tasks.
- The money goes instead to "Kitchen of the Future 3" (faster cook times and table turns), kitchen display systems, and self-seating, ordering and payment.
- Context: sales were flat, traffic was falling, and an 8 % price increase was announced at the same time.

**Discussion questions**
1. Chili's reported that guests were more satisfied (see R3 in the course references), yet it paused the robot. What would the robot have had to show to beat a kitchen upgrade in Hochman's two-variable test?
2. Which costs of a robot programme sit outside the robot's price tag (training, tray redesign, IT, management attention)? Which of them would your dossier need to quantify?

## 2 · Henn-na Hotel removes about half of its 243 robots

- **Source:** [MR] "Japan's Henn na Hotel fires half its robot workforce", *Hotel Management*, 31.01.2019
- **Live link:** https://www.hotelmanagement.net/tech/japan-s-henn-na-hotel-fires-half-its-robot-workforce
- **Status:** blocked (HTTP 403 to automated checks).
- **Archived copy (OK):** https://web.archive.org/web/20191118213254/https://www.hotelmanagement.net/tech/japan-s-henn-na-hotel-fires-half-its-robot-workforce
- **Original report:** Alastair Gale, "Robot Hotel Loses Love for Robots", *Wall Street Journal*, January 2019. Paywalled (HTTP 401): https://www.wsj.com/articles/robot-hotel-loses-love-for-robots-11547484628
- **Open equivalents (OK):**
  - NPR *All Things Considered*, 28.01.2019, interview with the WSJ reporter (transcript): https://www.npr.org/2019/01/28/689473868/this-time-humans-triumph-over-robots-as-they-take-back-hotel-jobs
  - Gizmodo, 15.01.2019: https://gizmodo.com/robots-ruin-robot-hotel-1831772555
- **Type:** trade press article that summarises other media (*Hotel Management* cites Business Insider and the Mirror). None of these sources is a first-hand hotel report.

**Key facts as stated**
- The hotel opened in 2015 at Huis Ten Bosch, Nagasaki. It cut its workforce of 243 robots because the robots "failed to reduce costs or workload for its employees". It kept robots where they were effective. [Hotel Management]
- Guests complained that the in-room assistant "Churi" took snoring for commands and woke them up. The front-desk robots could not answer basic questions. Luggage robots also struggled. [Hotel Management]
- Human staff worked overtime to repair broken robots. [Hotel Management]
- The front-desk dinosaur robots could not copy passports, so a human had to finish the job. The robot porter could not reach most rooms because it cannot climb stairs or go outside. The seven human staff spent much of their time recharging robots. [NPR]
- The hotel started with about 80 robots and grew to 243. Owner Hideo Sawada: "When you actually use robots you realize there are places where they aren't needed – or just annoy people." [Gizmodo, quoting WSJ]
- The dinosaurs at reception kept their jobs. [NPR]

**Discussion questions**
1. List every task that the robots moved onto human staff (recharging, passport copies, repairs, answering questions). What does this list imply for a "staff hours saved" figure in a business case?
2. Henn-na aimed to replace staff; Chili's and Skylark framed their robots as helpers. Is the framing the real cause of the outcome, or were the tasks and the environment (stairs, weather, voice interaction) simply harder?

## 3 · Skylark Group rolls out 3,000 Pudu BellaBots

- **Source:** [VC] Pudu Robotics, "Revolutionizing Restaurant Service at Skylark Group with BellaBot", case study (undated)
- **Link:** https://www.pudurobotics.com/case-studies/skylark-bellabot (redirects to /en/case-studies/skylark-bellabot)
- **Status:** OK.
- **Type:** vendor marketing case study. There is no method section, and the survey size is given only as "eight stores".
- **Independent cross-check (OK):** [MR] Robotstart (Japanese robotics trade press), 22.12.2022, reporting Skylark Holdings' own announcement: https://robotstart.info/2022/12/22/skylark-3000robots.html

**Key facts as stated**
- 3,000 BellaBots in more than 2,000 stores in Japan. [VC Pudu]
- "67 % of survey respondents expressing satisfaction", from an initial survey in eight stores. [VC Pudu]
- Claimed benefits: several dishes per trip, shorter service times, less walking and lifting for staff, and social-media attention for the cat design. No figures are given for time or labour savings. [VC Pudu]
- Skylark began the rollout in August 2021 and completed 3,000 robots in about 2,100 stores (Gusto, Shabu-yo, Bamiyan and others) on 27.12.2022. [MR Robotstart]
- Dedicated robot instructors visited every store and adjusted operations based on feedback from guests and staff. A customer survey covered 67 stores (n = 2,513); its results are published as images and were **not verified** here. [MR Robotstart]
- Figures circulating online (+2 % peak turnover, −42 % walking distance, −35 % clearing time) could not be traced to a primary document: **not verified**.

**Discussion questions**
1. Pudu reports an eight-store survey; Skylark reports a 67-store survey with 2,513 responses. What do you need to know about each survey before you quote "67 % satisfied"?
2. Skylark ran more than 2,000 stores with standardised layouts and menus. Which of these conditions does the HSLU cafeteria share, and which does it lack?

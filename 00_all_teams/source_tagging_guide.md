# Source tagging guide

DITEC HS26 · for the dossier, the case card and every presentation slide with numbers

Service-robot deployments are documented mostly by the people who sell them. A number is only as good as
its source – so **every number you use carries a tag**, and the rubric rewards triangulation: the same fact
confirmed by sources of different types.

## The tags

| Tag | Who says it | Typical example | How far to trust it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[vendor]** | The robot manufacturer | Pudu case study: "67 % customer satisfaction" | Marketing – the best case, often without method |
| **[integrator]** | Distributor or reseller | RobotLAB: "Servi about USD 15/day" | Sales arguments, useful for prices |
| **[company]** | The operator (restaurant chain, hotel) | Brinker: "82 % of guests report a better experience" | Own interest, but closer to reality than the vendor |
| **[press]** | Journalists, trade magazines | Restaurant Business on the Chili's pause | Varies – check whether they only repeat a press release |
| **[study]** | Peer-reviewed or independent research | Mejia et al. 2024 on restaurant staff | Strongest for "why" questions; check sample and setting |
| **[official]** | Statistics offices, collective agreements, laws | BFS wage survey, L-GAV minimum wages | Strong for context figures |
| **[own]** | Your observation or interview | "12 deliveries in 60 minutes, 2 interventions" | Strong if you describe the method |

## How to tag

- **In text:** "Skylark planned 3,000 BellaBots [vendor: Pudu case study, 2021]".
- **On slides:** a short tag after the number, the full reference in the source line at the bottom.
- **Chains:** "Pudu reports that Skylark surveyed guests" is [vendor] reporting [company] – tag the outer source and name the chain.
- **Your own calculations:** "≈ CHF 29 per staff hour [own calculation from official: L-GAV 2026]".

## Triangulate

- **Claims:** for each key claim (efficiency, labour, 24/7, experience), look for a second source of a different type.
- **Contradictions:** if the sources contradict each other, report both and explain the difference (e.g. pilot vs roll-out, survey question, time).
- **Status:** check whether the deployment still exists – a later press report, reviews, a phone call. "It was removed" is a finding.

## Red flags

- **No method:** a percentage without the question asked or the sample size.
- **Unclear date:** figures from 2020–21 used as if they described today.
- **Repackaged press releases:** the same wording across five articles is one source, not five.
- **Untraceable figures:** an ROI or payback claim with no cost basis.

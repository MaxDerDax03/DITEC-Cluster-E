# Common pool F · Supermarkets

Source pack for all teams · links checked 13.09.2026 · DITEC HS26

## Why this pool matters for every team
Supermarket floor robots meet thousands of customers every day without serving anyone. Badger's "Marty" is 6 ft 7.5 in tall and was first seen as frightening; a store associate stuck googly eyes on it, and it became a mascot. A vendor-commissioned survey says shoppers prefer the look of Simbe's Tally, and a TikTok claim that Marty spies on shoppers went viral. These cases isolate three questions every team faces: how the robot looks, how staff and shoppers read its purpose, and what its cameras capture. Walmart's exit from Bossa Nova shows that shelf-scanning robots can also be cut.

## Go deeper – additional sources

## Starter questions for your dossier
1. **Context:** store size, aisle width, opening hours and when the robot runs (5 a.m. vs peak).
2. **Robot:** height, camera count, lidar, speed – compare Marty, Tally and your TurtleBot 4.
3. **Business model:** US$35,000 per unit (press, 2019) vs RaaS; retailer data as the real product.
4. **Operational evidence:** "40 hazards per store per day" and "650 robots" are vendor figures; look for any independent audit.
5. **Staff and customer response:** fear, mascot, surveillance rumour; staff paged for twist-ties ([staff acceptance briefing](../staff_acceptance_of_robots.md)).
6. **Failure modes:** false positives, perceived surveillance, labour conflict, the retailer finding a cheaper human process (Walmart).
7. **Transfer to the HSLU cafeteria:** requirements for approachable design (height, eyes, lights), visible data-privacy signage, no image capture of people, and a legible "what am I doing" signal.

# Acceptance inside the team: how co-workers see service robots

Briefing for the case-study track and the week-3 input

**Why this matters.** Customer acceptance gets the headlines. Whether a robot survives month three
is decided by the servers, cooks and managers who work next to it: who fixes it, who charges it, who
feels replaced by it.

Source types: **[PR]** peer-reviewed · **[IR]** industry or international-organisation report ·
**[MR]** media report · **[VC]** vendor or company claim · **[Book]** academic book · **[Law]** statute

## 1 · What the evidence says about employees

- **Fear of being replaced has costs.** Employees who expect smart technology, AI, robots or algorithms to replace their jobs report lower commitment and career satisfaction, more cynicism, depression and intention to quit. [PR] Brougham & Haar, *Journal of Management & Organization* 24(2), 2018 – https://doi.org/10.1017/jmo.2016.55
- **The same holds in hotels.** In 468 five-star hotel employees, awareness of AI and robotics went with turnover intention; perceived organisational support changed how strong the effect was. [PR] Li, Bonn & Ye, *Tourism Management*, 2019 – https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tourman.2019.02.006
- **Performance up, passion down.** Robot adoption raised frontline hotel staff's service performance but reduced their "service passion" when staff felt treated as replaceable tools. [PR] Xu et al., *International Journal of Hospitality Management*, 2025 – https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhm.2025.104162
- **Staff working with delivery robots** (42 US restaurant workers, about a year of use, robots introduced by management):
  - They liked the reduced physical strain, and guests found the robot entertaining.
  - They disliked glitches and slowness, too little training and manager support, and having no voice in the introduction.
  - Some reported resentment and job fears, especially older workers.
  - [PR] Mejia, Crandell, Broker & Shoss, *Journal of Hospitality and Tourism Technology* 15(5), 2024 – https://doi.org/10.1108/JHTT-12-2023-0420
- **Workload decides.** The same hospital delivery robot was rejected on a busy ward and accepted on a calmer one. [PR] Mutlu & Forlizzi, HRI 2008 – https://doi.org/10.1145/1349822.1349860
- **Perceptions change over time** – from "alien" to machine to work partner. [PR] Ljungblad et al., CSCW 2012 – https://doi.org/10.1145/2145204.2145233

## 2 · The Japan angle – naming robots

- **Staff named the robots themselves.** At a Gyukaku restaurant, servers nicknamed their two Servi robots "Oyamada-kun A/B" and called them colleagues. [MR] Nikkei Business, 2021 – https://business.nikkei.com/atcl/NBD/19/00109/00142/
- **Naming is not specifically Japanese:** Rita (Chili's), Maple (Denny's), Bella (Liebesbier, Bayreuth), Hilde and Layla in German restaurants.
- **Names help when they grow from the team.** In a four-year study of European factories, workers named robots ("Matilda is lazy today"), which helped them cope and troubleshoot – "it's not something they can be told to do". [PR] Goštautaitė, Reay et al., *Group & Organization Management*, 2026 – https://doi.org/10.1177/10596011261428932
- **"Japanese animism" is a contested explanation.**
  - Japanese survey respondents were less positive about robots than the stereotype assumes. [PR] Bartneck et al., *AI & Society*, 2007 – https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-006-0052-7
  - The idea of a special Japanese "robot culture" is actively promoted by roboticists and policymakers. [PR] Šabanović, *Social Studies of Science*, 2014 – https://doi.org/10.1177/0306312713509704
- **The stronger driver is labour shortage:** more than 60 % of Japanese hotels and ryokans report it. [IR] Teikoku Databank via HotelBank, 2024 – https://en.hotelbank.jp/news/teikoku-databank-survey-recovery-in-tourism-demand-but-labor-shortages-challenge-the-ryokan-and-hotel-industry/
- **Limits:**
  - Very human-like robots raised employees' feeling of job threat and their resistance. [PR] Tojib et al., *Journal of Service Management*, 2023 – https://doi.org/10.1108/JOSM-03-2022-0090
  - Human-like features both energised staff and increased job threat. [PR] Liu et al., *Scientific Reports*, 2025 – https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-05405-2
  - In Japanese elder care, robots added work. [Book] Wright, *Robots Won't Save Japan*, Cornell UP, 2023 – https://www.cornellpress.cornell.edu/book/9781501768040/robots-wont-save-japan/
  - Henn-na Hotel removed about half of its 243 robots because they created work. [MR] https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/346/
- No documented workplace robot ceremonies or uniforms in Japanese hospitality were found (robot funerals exist for consumer robots such as aibo). [Book] Robertson, *Robo sapiens japanicus*, 2018.

## 3 · Deployment cases

- **Liebesbier, Bayreuth:** the team was hesitant at first because guest contact matters to them; the head chef says integration was quick "because Bella is an enormous relief". Stated goal: support, not replace. [VC] https://www.liebesbier.de/de/news/ciao-bella-service-roboter-unterstuetzt-im-liebesbier-1607/
- **Fischen im Allgäu and Dormitz, Bavaria:** staff first asked whether their jobs were at risk; they came round because of less heavy carrying and more time with guests. [IR] BZT Bayern, 2023 – https://bzt.bayern/arbeitskraeftemangel-serviceroboter-gastronomie/
- **Chili's "Rita":** the company reports that 84 % of team members were enthusiastic – its own survey. [VC/MR] https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/chilis-will-bring-restaurant-robot-to-51-more-stores/622183/
- **Denny's, Fresno:** "It's not here to take a job, it is to enhance the employee's job." [MR] https://abc30.com/post/dennys-robot-server/11789912/
- **Andulino and Verkehrshaus, Switzerland 2021:** no jobs cut; the robot takes the long walking routes. [MR] https://www.htr.ch/story/gastronomie/erste-serviceroboter-in-der-schweizer-gastronomie-32414.html
- **Unions:** no specific position of ver.di, NGG or Unia on service robots in hospitality was found – a gap your field work could fill.

## 4 · What raises staff acceptance

| Measure | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Staff decide routes and tasks; trial before buying | [PR] Karlsen et al., *Frontiers in Robotics and AI*, 2026 – https://doi.org/10.3389/frobt.2026.1793138 |
| Frame the robot as a carrying aid (health and safety), not as headcount reduction | Karlsen 2026; Liebesbier; BZT Bayern |
| Consult worker representatives and train staff | [IR] OECD *Employment Outlook* 2023 – https://doi.org/10.1787/08785bba-en |
| Clear job-security message and visible organisational support | Li et al. 2019; Liu et al. 2025 |
| Train the new workflow, not just the buttons | Mejia et al. 2024; Karlsen 2026 |
| Let names and relationships grow from the team; avoid very human-like designs | Goštautaitė et al. 2026; Tojib et al. 2023 |
| Match the rollout to workload – do not start at the busiest shift | Mutlu & Forlizzi 2008 |
| Keep guest contact with the staff | Xu et al. 2025 |
| Co-determination and no performance monitoring | [Law] Germany BetrVG §90 and §87(1) no. 6 – https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__87.html · Switzerland ArGV 3 Art. 26 – https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1993/2564_2564_2564/de |
| Worker voice as a success factor | [IR] MIT Work of the Future, Kochan 2020 – https://workofthefuture-taskforce.mit.edu/research-post/worker-voice-representation-and-implications-for-public-policies/ |

Not supported by evidence found so far: sharing productivity gains through tips or bonuses (anecdotes only), "robot champions", ADKAR or Kotter applied to service robots.

## 5 · In this course

- **Dossier question (all teams):** who carries the extra work – fixing, recharging, recovering from failures – and who gets the gains? How were staff involved before go-live?
- **Requirements your robot can meet and test** (candidates for the hinge table):
  - Yields to people: stops at ≥ 0.5 m and waits or re-routes – 10 crossings, 0 contacts.
  - Announces its intent before moving (light or sound) – predictable motion is easier to work around (Dragan, Lee & Srinivasa, HRI 2013 – https://doi.org/10.1109/HRI.2013.6483603).
  - Staff can pause the robot or send it to the dock with a button in ≤ 2 s – the TurtleBot 4 has four buttons.
  - Logs deliveries, not the behaviour of individual staff members.
- **Week 3 role play (30'):**
  - Roles: manager, a sceptical 55-year-old server, a works-council member, the chef.
  - The manager announces a BellaBot; observers score the announcement with the table in section 4.
  - Debrief with the Tojib and Goštautaitė findings.

## Caveats

- Most attitude studies are cross-sectional surveys, many from Chinese hotels.
- Mejia et al. 2024 is the only in-depth study of restaurant staff working with delivery robots.
- Deployment cases are told by managers or vendors; staff are rarely quoted directly.
- Some paywalled sources were read through abstracts or summaries (Mutlu & Forlizzi 2008, Xu et al. 2025).
